The fog had not lifted for three days. Captain Ilse Moreau stood at the rail while young Tomas heaved the chip log over the stern and counted under his breath.

"Seven knots," he called.

Seven knots for three days, Ilse thought, and not one glimpse of the sun. Her dead reckoning said they were forty leagues west of the Lizard, but she had stopped trusting it on the second night. Below, the chronometer ticked in its padded box, keeping Greenwich time for a meridian none of them could see.

At eight bells the watch changed. Ilse took the sextant from its case and waited for the sky to open.
